Gym
CHEST

Barbell Bench Press

4 × 8–10 Intermediate
  1. Lie on the bench with eyes under the bar, feet flat on the floor.
  2. Grip slightly wider than shoulder-width and unrack the bar over your chest.
  3. Lower the bar under control to mid-chest, elbows at roughly 45°.
  4. Press back up to full lock-out without bouncing off your chest.

Common mistake: flaring the elbows to 90° — it stresses the shoulders. Keep them tucked closer to your torso.

CHEST

Push-Up

3 × 15–20 Beginner
  1. Start in a plank with hands slightly wider than shoulders.
  2. Keep a straight line from head to heels — brace your core.
  3. Lower your chest to just above the floor, elbows at ~45°.
  4. Push through your palms back to the start.

Common mistake: letting the hips sag. Squeeze your glutes and abs to keep the line straight.

Gym
CHEST

Incline Dumbbell Press

3 × 10–12 Intermediate
  1. Set the bench to a 30–45° incline and sit with a dumbbell in each hand.
  2. Press the dumbbells up above your upper chest until arms are extended.
  3. Lower slowly until upper arms are roughly parallel to the floor.
  4. Press back up, keeping wrists stacked over elbows.

Common mistake: setting the incline too steep — above 45° it shifts the work to the shoulders.

BACK

Deadlift

4 × 5–6 Advanced
  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, bar over mid-foot.
  2. Hinge at the hips and grip the bar just outside your knees.
  3. Flatten your back, brace your core, and lift by driving through the floor.
  4. Stand tall at the top, then lower the bar back down under control.

Common mistake: rounding the lower back. If your back rounds, lighten the load until form is solid.

Home
BACK

Pull-Up

4 × Max reps Intermediate
  1. Hang from the bar with an overhand grip, slightly wider than shoulders.
  2. Pull your chest up toward the bar by driving your elbows down.
  3. Get your chin over the bar without swinging.
  4. Lower back to a full dead hang under control.

Common mistake: using momentum. Slow the descent down — that's where most of the muscle work happens.

Gym
BACK

Bent-Over Row

4 × 8–10 Intermediate
  1. Hinge at the hips to about 45°, holding the bar with an overhand grip.
  2. Keep your back flat and core braced throughout.
  3. Pull the bar toward your lower ribs, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  4. Lower with control back to the start.

Common mistake: standing too upright, which turns the row into a shrug. Keep the hinge.

Gym
SHOULDERS

Overhead Press

4 × 6–8 Intermediate
  1. Hold the bar at shoulder height, grip just outside shoulder-width.
  2. Brace your core and glutes — don't lean back.
  3. Press the bar straight up, moving your head back slightly to let it pass.
  4. Lock out overhead, then lower back to the shoulders.

Common mistake: over-arching the lower back. Squeeze your glutes to keep the ribs stacked over the hips.

Gym
SHOULDERS

Lateral Raise

3 × 12–15 Beginner
  1. Stand tall holding a light dumbbell in each hand at your sides.
  2. With a soft bend in the elbows, raise the dumbbells out to the sides.
  3. Stop at shoulder height — no higher.
  4. Lower slowly back to the start.

Common mistake: swinging the weight up with momentum. Go lighter and control the tempo.

Gym
SHOULDERS

Face Pull

3 × 15 Beginner
  1. Set a cable or band at upper-chest height with a rope attachment.
  2. Pull the rope toward your face, splitting your hands apart at the end.
  3. Focus on squeezing your rear shoulders and upper back.
  4. Return slowly to the start.

Common mistake: using too much weight and turning it into a low row. Keep it light and controlled.

Gym
ARMS

Barbell Curl

3 × 10–12 Beginner
  1. Stand tall holding the bar with an underhand, shoulder-width grip.
  2. Keep your elbows pinned to your sides.
  3. Curl the bar up toward your shoulders, squeezing the biceps.
  4. Lower slowly back to a full stretch.

Common mistake: swinging the hips to heave the bar up. Keep the movement strict.

Home
ARMS

Hammer Curl

3 × 12 Beginner
  1. Hold a dumbbell in each hand with palms facing your body.
  2. Keep elbows tucked and curl the weights up together or alternating.
  3. Squeeze at the top without rotating your wrists.
  4. Lower under control.

Common mistake: letting the elbows drift forward, which shifts tension off the arms.

Gym
ARMS

Triceps Pushdown

3 × 12–15 Beginner
  1. Stand facing a cable stack with a straight or rope attachment set high.
  2. Pin your elbows to your sides throughout.
  3. Push the attachment down until your arms are fully extended.
  4. Let it rise back slowly under control.

Common mistake: letting the elbows flare out and away from the torso, which recruits the shoulders instead.

LEGS

Back Squat

4 × 6–8 Advanced
  1. Rest the bar on your upper back, feet shoulder-width apart.
  2. Brace your core and unrack the bar, taking a couple steps back.
  3. Sit down and back, keeping knees tracking over your toes.
  4. Go to at least parallel, then drive back up through your heels.

Common mistake: letting the knees cave inward on the way up. Actively push them out.

Home
LEGS

Walking Lunge

3 × 12 per leg Beginner
  1. Stand tall, take a controlled step forward.
  2. Lower until both knees are bent around 90°, back knee hovering above the floor.
  3. Push through the front heel to step into the next lunge.
  4. Keep your torso upright throughout.

Common mistake: taking too short a step, which puts extra stress on the front knee.

Gym
LEGS

Romanian Deadlift

4 × 8–10 Intermediate
  1. Hold the bar at hip height with a shoulder-width grip.
  2. With a slight knee bend, push your hips back and lower the bar down your thighs.
  3. Keep the bar close and your back flat until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings.
  4. Drive your hips forward to return to standing.

Common mistake: turning it into a squat by bending the knees too much. This is a hip-hinge, not a knee-bend.

ABS

Plank

3 × 40–60s Beginner
  1. Rest on your forearms and toes, elbows under your shoulders.
  2. Form a straight line from head to heels.
  3. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
  4. Breathe steadily and hold the position.

Common mistake: letting the hips rise up or sag down. Check your line in a mirror if you can.

Gym
ABS

Hanging Leg Raise

3 × 10–12 Intermediate
  1. Hang from a pull-up bar with arms fully extended.
  2. Brace your core and raise your legs until roughly parallel to the floor.
  3. Avoid swinging — control the movement both ways.
  4. Lower slowly back to the start.

Common mistake: using momentum to swing the legs up. Slow it down and lead with your lower abs.

Home
ABS

Russian Twist

3 × 20 twists Beginner
  1. Sit with knees bent, lean back slightly to engage your core.
  2. Lift your feet off the floor if you can, holding a weight or clasped hands.
  3. Rotate your torso to tap the floor on each side.
  4. Keep the movement controlled, not fast and sloppy.

Common mistake: rotating only the arms instead of the torso. The twist should come from your core.

Home
CARDIO

HIIT Sprint Intervals

8 × 30s on / 60s off Intermediate
  1. Warm up with 5 minutes of easy jogging.
  2. Sprint at a hard, sustainable effort for 30 seconds.
  3. Walk or jog slowly for 60 seconds to recover.
  4. Repeat, then cool down with light walking.

Common mistake: skipping the warm-up. Sprinting on cold muscles raises injury risk.

Home
CARDIO

Jump Rope

5 × 2 min Beginner
  1. Hold the handles lightly, elbows close to your sides.
  2. Turn the rope mainly with your wrists, not your arms.
  3. Jump just high enough to clear the rope.
  4. Land softly on the balls of your feet.

Common mistake: jumping too high. Small, quick hops are more efficient and easier on the knees.

Gym
CARDIO

Rowing Machine

20 min steady Beginner
  1. Drive through your legs first, then lean back slightly.
  2. Finish by pulling the handle to your lower ribs.
  3. Reverse the order on the way back: arms, then body, then legs.
  4. Keep a steady, sustainable pace.

Common mistake: pulling with the arms first. Legs should always initiate the drive.

Set 05 — Coaching Notes

Training tips that actually matter

The fundamentals that separate steady progress from spinning your wheels.

Stay Hydrated

Drink water before, during, and after training — even mild dehydration hurts performance.

Proper Form First

Never sacrifice technique for heavier weight. Master the movement, then add load.

Always Warm Up

Spend at least 10 minutes raising your heart rate and mobilizing the joints you'll train.

Prioritize Recovery

Muscle is built during rest. Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep and don't skip rest days.

Progressive Overload

Gradually increase weight, reps, or sets over time — your body only adapts to new demands.

Listen to Pain

Muscle fatigue is normal; sharp joint pain is not. Stop and reassess if something feels wrong.
